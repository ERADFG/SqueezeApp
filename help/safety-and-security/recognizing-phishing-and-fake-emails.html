Recognizing phishing and fake emails

Article 8 of 10 in Safety and security

Phishing attempts often imitate InteractInk's look and tone specifically to trick you into handing over your password. A few reliable warning signs to watch for:

  • Urgent language pushing you to log in immediately or risk losing access to your account.
  • Links that don't actually point to interactink.com when you hover over or long-press them.
  • Requests for your password sent directly by email or DM — InteractInk will never ask for your password this way, under any circumstance.

If you receive a message like this, the safest move is simple: don't click the link at all. Instead, open a new browser tab and go to the site directly by typing the address yourself, then log in there if you want to check your account status. If the suspicious message came through InteractInk's own messaging system rather than external email, report it using the same reporting tools you'd use for any other post or message, so the account sending it can be reviewed.
